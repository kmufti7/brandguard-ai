# PDR-001: Why Fail-Closed Safety Gates

## Context

BrandGuard AI operates in the telecommunications marketing domain, where Strand Wireless campaigns must comply with FCC regulations, consumer protection statutes, and internal brand standards. Prior to this decision, the team faced a fundamental architectural choice: how should the release boundary handle safety validation failures?

The core tension emerged from two observable failure modes:

1. **False positives**: Valid marketing copy (factually accurate, on-brand, compliant) blocked by overly conservative safety rules. This creates friction for marketing teams and slows campaign velocity.
2. **False negatives**: Problematic copy (factually misleading, off-brand, potentially regulatory risk) shipped to production. This exposes Strand Wireless to customer trust erosion, regulatory inquiry, and reputational harm.

At the time of this decision, DJ-002 had established that all safety gates must be deterministic (not probabilistic), eliminating the option of confidence-threshold-based allow-through logic. The decision to codify the P22 governance standard, captured in DJ-006, created a clear legal and operational benchmark against which all content could be measured.

The question was not whether to gate releases, but whether to fail open (allow release if validation is uncertain) or fail closed (block release unless validation confirms safety).

## Decision

**BrandGuard AI will implement fail-closed safety gates at all release boundaries.** No content moves from draft to production unless the deterministic Verifier confirms compliance with governance rules. If verification fails, the release is blocked. Marketing teams receive actionable feedback and can resubmit after remediation.

This decision applies uniformly across all content types (email, SMS, web copy, social media, display ads) and all channels through which Strand Wireless campaigns are distributed.

## Trade-Offs Considered

### False Positives vs. False Negatives

The team modeled two scenarios:

**Scenario A: Fail-Open (Allow-Through on Validation Uncertainty)**
- Reduces short-term friction: marketing launches 15-20% faster when rules are unclear or overly strict.
- Increases false negative risk: 1-3% of shipped content might violate brand standards or governance policy per monthly campaign volume (estimated 50-100 campaigns/month at scale).
- Customer impact: A single "Strand Wireless offers unlimited 5G in all coverage areas" claim (factually unsupported) reaching 500,000 subscribers erodes trust, invites FCC inquiry, and requires costly correction campaigns.
- Regulatory exposure: Documented safety-gate failures create liability in discovery and suggest negligent governance.
- Recovery cost: Retracting a shipped campaign costs 3-5x more than blocking it pre-release.

**Scenario B: Fail-Closed (Block Unless Verified Safe)**
- Increases short-term friction: marketing teams experience delays when rules are overly strict or ambiguous. Estimated 5-10% of submissions require one remediation cycle (48-72 hours).
- Eliminates false negative risk: no problematic content ships without explicit human review override (which requires documented justification).
- Customer impact: Delayed launch of a compliant campaign has zero reputational cost and preserves brand trust.
- Regulatory posture: Fail-closed gates create an audit trail showing systematic governance and reduce liability exposure.
- Recovery cost: Clarifying a rule or relaxing overly strict validation costs labor and process review, but does not expose Strand Wireless to customer harm.

The team assessed the financial trade-off: one false-negative shipping incident (regulatory investigation, customer-trust repair, earned media damage) costs $500K-$2M in direct and indirect costs. One false-positive delay (48-72 hour remediation cycle) costs $5K-$15K in internal labor and opportunity cost. The ratio strongly favors fail-closed behavior.

### Audit Defensibility

Strand Wireless is subject to FCC oversight and state-level consumer protection enforcement. Regulators expect companies to document safety controls. A fail-closed architecture creates a clear, reviewable record: every shipped campaign passed deterministic verification. A fail-open architecture creates defensive liability: "Why did you allow this content through when validation flagged risk?" Fail-closed is the only posture that survives regulatory interrogation.

## Foreclosed Paths

This decision explicitly rules out:

- **Confidence-threshold gating**: Per DJ-002, probabilistic gates are not permitted. Fail-closed gates must be deterministic.
- **Human-override-at-release**: Allowing marketing leaders to ship content flagged by Verifier without documented legal review. This is permitted only via formal exception process with governance sign-off.
- **Soft-fail messaging**: Warnings that content "might" violate rules but allow release anyway. Such messaging creates false sense of safety and obscures accountability.
- **Async remediation after ship**: Allowing content to go live with known issues and "fixing it later." This forecloses regulatory defensibility entirely.

## Consequences

### Operational Impact

Marketing teams will experience 5-10% longer cycle times for campaign launches. The team must invest in rule clarity (fewer ambiguous gates), template libraries (reducing the need for custom compliance review), and escalation processes (allowing urgent, low-risk campaigns to move faster through documented exception workflows).

### Governance Maturity

Fail-closed gates force the team to articulate governance rules with precision. Vague rules will generate false positives, which will create pressure to clarify or relax rules explicitly (not quietly). This drives governance to become increasingly specific and defensible over time.

### Brand Trust

By blocking non-compliant content pre-release, BrandGuard AI ensures Strand Wireless marketing maintains factual accuracy and regulatory alignment. Customer-facing claims are always verified. This compounds brand trust over quarters and years, supporting long-term customer retention and regulatory relationships.

### Future Integration

Fail-closed gates establish the foundation for integrating additional validators (accessibility checkers, tone analysis, localization risk) without requiring architectural rework. Each new validator can inherit the same fail-closed discipline.
