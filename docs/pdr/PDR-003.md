# PDR-003: Why Single Fictional Brand and Five SKUs

## Context

BrandGuard AI is a governed marketing AI product that requires a public-facing portfolio demonstrating audience discovery and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) capabilities on authentic brand and product data. Early in the project, the team faced a fundamental scope question: should the portfolio use real brand partnerships, multiple synthetic brands, or a single carefully constructed fictional brand with a bounded product lineup?

At the time of this decision (Session 4), we had completed DJ-001, which established that a synthetic-only corpus would be the substrate for all portfolio work. That decision ruled out real brand partnerships and their attendant legal, contractual, and brand-safety risks. The remaining question was how to structure the fictional layer: one brand or many, and how many products per brand.

The BrandGuard AI team needed to balance three competing pressures. First, the portfolio had to be substantial enough to demonstrate real RAG capabilities: a trivially small dataset would not convince stakeholders that the system could scale. Second, the decision needed to keep implementation scope bounded so that Session 4 and Session 5 deliverables would finish on time and within engineering hours. Third, the fictional brand itself had to feel authentic and credible to reviewers, not like a transparent placeholder.

The audience discovery pipeline required diverse content across product tiers, use cases, and marketing collateral types. The governance gates required enough SKU variety to test legal review, compliance mapping, and tone consistency across different product personas. Too few SKUs would leave gaps; too many would multiply the content generation workload without proportional benefit.

## Decision

We chose Strand Wireless, a fictional United States telecom operator, as the sole brand for all BrandGuard AI portfolio work. Strand Wireless operates exactly five SKUs: Essentials, Pro, Family, Unlimited+, and Business. All audience discovery work, RAG pipeline testing, and governance gate validation use this one brand and these five products exclusively.

Strand Wireless was designed to reflect a mid-market regional carrier with national ambitions. Its brand voice, documented in data/brand_voice.md, combines clarity and directness with a focus on reliability and practical value. This voice is distinct enough from major incumbents (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) that it reads as authentic but not derivative. The five SKUs span individual prepaid and postpaid offerings (Essentials, Pro), family and household plans (Family, Unlimited+), and commercial segments (Business), creating natural boundaries for marketing strategy and messaging variation.

This structure creates seven interdependent content domains: one brand voice, five product briefs, and one unified governance framework. The portfolio deliverables reference this structure directly and exhaustively. No other brands are created. No products are added to Strand Wireless after this decision.

## Trade-Offs Considered

**Single brand vs. multi-brand portfolio:** A portfolio with three to five fictional brands would have demonstrated broader system capabilities and created richer stress tests for the governance gates. Multi-brand work would have let us showcase tone consistency checks across different voice profiles and industry segments. However, each additional brand requires its own brand voice document, legal review gate calibration, and core messaging framework. For a public portfolio with Session 4 and Session 5 deadlines, the engineering and documentation effort increases superlinearly with brand count. A single, well-realized brand allows us to spend more time on depth of RAG testing and governance sophistication rather than breadth of brand variety. Reviewers evaluating a portfolio piece prefer one brand executed excellently over three brands executed adequately.

**Five SKUs vs. larger or smaller lineups:** A three-SKU lineup (Essentials, Pro, Business) would have reduced content generation work and fit tightly within a two-week sprint. However, three SKUs do not cover the consumer family segment or the high-end unlimited tier, both of which generate distinct marketing challenges and legal complexity. A ten-SKU lineup would create richer product taxonomy work and more realistic enterprise scenarios. However, generating marketing collateral, fact sheets, and governance artifacts for ten distinct products would have required an additional engineering week. Five SKUs hit the exact boundary where the audience discovery pipeline can be thoroughly tested without artificial constraint, and the governance gates face real product differentiation challenges (e.g., Family vs. Unlimited+ overlap in messaging, Business tier pricing opacity) without becoming unmaintainable.

**Synthetic corpus only vs. real brand partnerships:** DJ-001 already decided this in favor of synthetic content. Real brand partnerships would have generated authentic marketing materials and real governance requirements, but would have introduced contractual lock-in, brand-safety liability, and approval delays. A synthetic corpus eliminates that friction and lets us publish freely in a public portfolio without partner sign-off.

## Foreclosed Paths

This decision rules out:

- Creating additional fictional brands after PDR-003 acceptance. Any new brand would require revision of the governance framework, retesting of the compliance mapping gates, and expansion of the RAG corpus. Portfolio deliverables are scoped to Strand Wireless only.
- Expanding the Strand Wireless SKU lineup to more than five products during Sessions 4 or 5. Scope creep in product count directly delays audience discovery demos and governance validation. If future product extensions are needed, they require a separate decision record and a new development phase.
- Retroactively adopting real brand data from actual telecom operators. Synthetic corpus work creates a clean legal and ethical boundary; mixing real and synthetic materials would blur that boundary and require re-review by legal and brand-safety teams.
- Positioning Strand Wireless as a real company or implying that BrandGuard AI has a partnership with any actual telecom entity. All portfolio materials must clearly label Strand Wireless as a fictional construct created for demonstration purposes.

## Consequences

**Immediate downstream effects:**

- The audience discovery pipeline is scoped to index exactly five product SKUs across one brand voice. Search queries in the portfolio demo return results filtered to Strand Wireless alone. This simplifies RAG validation but requires clear documentation that production systems would scale to arbitrary brand counts.
- The governance gates (legal_brand_review_gate, compliance_mapping_gate, tone_consistency_gate, and similar modules in src/brandguard/governance/) are calibrated against Strand Wireless content and tested against Strand Wireless workflows. Portal reviewers will see governance artifacts labeled with Strand Wireless identifiers. Production customization to new brands is not demonstrated in the portfolio.
- Content generation (marketing copy, fact sheets, compliance narratives) is bounded to five product briefs. This eliminates variability in downstream gate testing and creates reproducible test scenarios.

**Stakeholder and communication impact:**

- Portfolio reviewers (investors, enterprise prospects, technical evaluators) will see Strand Wireless repeatedly. Clarity about fictional status is essential in all external materials. The product story is "BrandGuard AI governs marketing content for Strand Wireless" not "BrandGuard AI can govern any brand instantly."
- Internal stakeholders (engineering, product, design) have a single, concrete reference brand. This reduces design ambiguity and speeds decision-making on tone, governance rules, and RAG query behavior.
- Future customers will understand that their own brand onboarding requires them to provide brand voice, product taxonomy, and governance policies. Strand Wireless is a complete worked example, not a template they can reuse.

**Long-term architectural implications:**

- The five-SKU constraint creates a testable, maintainable scope boundary for the Session 4 and Session 5 portfolio. Scaling to ten SKUs or three brands remains a documented future option but is not attempted now.
- All governance gate logic is written to be brand-agnostic and SKU-count-agnostic, even though testing uses only Strand Wireless. This design discipline ensures that production customization for real brands does not require gate rewrites.
- The synthetic corpus remains fully under team control. No dependency on external brand owners or approval processes. This agility is a competitive advantage for the portfolio timeline.
