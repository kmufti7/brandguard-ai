# PDR-004: Why Eval Before Integration

## Context

When BrandGuard AI entered the integration phase in late Q4, the team faced a sequencing choice: ship connectors to Salesforce and Marketo first, then build measurement infrastructure afterward, or establish a formal evaluation harness and golden dataset before any external system touch-points. The pressure for early integration was real. Customer pilots were waiting. Sales had timelines.

At that moment, the product was functionally complete for core governance tasks (content review, brand violation detection, compliance scoring). The natural instinct in product development is to validate value through real-world usage. But BrandGuard is not a feature addition to an existing platform. It is a governance layer that makes decisions about marketing content. Those decisions carry legal and reputational weight for customers. Adoption of such a product does not depend on feature breadth. It depends on trust [brand_voice:§2].

Trust in a governance product is not aspirational. It is measurable. And measurement must precede integration, not follow it.

## Decision

BrandGuard will ship an eval harness and golden dataset (20 scenarios covering trademark, compliance, content policy, and tone misalignments) before releasing any Salesforce, Marketo, or other external system integrations. The harness will execute repeatable tests against the governance engine. The golden dataset will serve as the deterministic baseline for all future performance claims. Operational SLAs (throughput and latency) derived from Session 4.1 will be published alongside the eval results and will govern integration design choices.

Only after eval infrastructure is operational and results are documented will integration work begin.

## Trade-Offs Considered

**Integration-first (rejected):** Release Salesforce and Marketo connectors in Q4, measure real-world performance in customer pilots, iterate on governance accuracy based on production signals. Benefits: faster time to revenue, real-world signal, customer feedback. Drawbacks: BrandGuard decisions in production before we have a controlled baseline; no way to distinguish product defects from customer misconfiguration; customers see variable accuracy in early pilots; trust erodes before it can form; integration code becomes coupled to undocumented behavior; premature optimization of connectors to work around governance issues rather than fixing the core engine [DJ-002]. This path risks shipping a product that "works for some customers" rather than a product customers can trust.

**Eval-first (chosen):** Build and publish the harness and golden dataset first. Customers see reproducible results before connecting BrandGuard to their systems. The 20-scenario dataset covers the most common governance failures observed in brand marketing. The throughput and latency numbers (Session 4.1) give customers realistic expectations for SLA design. Benefits: customers can audit the product's reasoning before integration; no surprises in production; integration design can target known operational constraints; governance accuracy is not a mystery; trust is earned before revenue is at stake. Drawback: three-to-four week delay in Salesforce/Marketo availability.

The signals that favored eval-first: (1) BrandGuard governance decisions are not reversible in real time (a brand violation flagged in a customer's campaign may already have been scheduled); (2) the product's value proposition is prediction accuracy and compliance coverage, not speed or feature count; (3) customers care more about why a decision was made than whether integrations exist.

## Foreclosed Paths

This decision forecloses any release of external system integrations before the eval harness is operational and golden dataset results are published. It also forecloses the integration-driven development model in which connectors are built first and governance logic is tuned to fit connector requirements.

By committing to eval-first, the team also forecloses rapid iteration on governance rules based on individual customer feedback in early pilots. Instead, rule changes will be validated against the golden dataset and tested in the harness before release. This is slower but more defensible.

## Consequences

**Immediate (Weeks 1-4):** Eval harness development proceeds in parallel with integration design, not sequentially. The 20-scenario dataset is formalized and published. Throughput/latency SLAs are documented (reference Session 4.1 data). Customer timelines for Salesforce and Marketo connectors slip 3 to 4 weeks.

**Medium-term (Months 2-3):** Integration work begins with known operational and accuracy constraints. Connectors are designed to pass the golden dataset tests, not to work around governance defects. The eval harness becomes the acceptance gate for all future governance changes.

**Long-term:** Trust accumulates. Customers adopt BrandGuard not because the connectors are easiest to install, but because they can verify the product's reasoning. Future integrations (downstream analytics platforms, approval workflows) are built on a foundation of proven accuracy rather than aspirational promises [DJ-008]. The eval harness becomes the source of truth for performance claims in sales and support conversations.

The trade-off is a delay in time-to-integration. The gain is that integration, when it ships, carries the weight of measurement. That is the correct sequencing for a governance product.
